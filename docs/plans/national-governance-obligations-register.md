# CivicMAPS national governance obligations register

Prepared 18 October 2026 (Australia/Melbourne) by Codex (agent), using official regulator, government and legislation sources checked on that date.

**Scope confirmed by the owner: all Australian states and territories, across the platform's intended data classes. Status: initial applicability and implementation register; detailed legal, contractual and council acceptance remains open.** This document supports the [governance gate](governance-and-production-readiness-gate.md). It does not approve production handling or establish exhaustive compliance with every departmental law.

## National approach

One national product needs jurisdiction-specific handling rules. A council hostname, database location or common privacy policy does not determine all applicable law. Identify the responsible public body, contracted operator, collection purpose, relevant individual/record, statutory power, jurisdiction, processing recipient and location for each flow. A shared-service arrangement can involve several organisations and jurisdictions; preserve the existing distinction between Tenant, Organisation, Jurisdiction, Data Plane and operating context.

The owner confirmed staff identities/employee details, public and service reports, infringements/notices, receipts, evidence/media, operational records, officer locations, health/sensitive information and audit trails. Assess incidental sensitive information in free text and attachments as well as designed fields. Classification in this register is not permission to collect it. Minimise each workflow and obtain its lawful authority before activation.

## Common controls and jurisdiction-specific rules

Use common mechanisms for access enforcement, provenance, legal holds, consent/notice records where required, protected audit, data minimisation, export and supplier incident cooperation. Bind the legally reviewed rules to jurisdiction, record class, workflow and effective date. A technical ability to share a map or administer two councils must not imply lawful sharing of personnel, complainants, health details or evidence.

Retention should select the applicable record class and event trigger, apply lawful holds and record authorised disposal. A longest-retention or shortest-retention rule applied blindly to every national record is not an adequate resolution of conflicting duties. Backups, secondary evidence, caches, mobile copies, telemetry and exports remain part of the inventory.

Departmental annexes must assess infringements/fines, statutory delegations, evidence/custody, surveillance and workplace/location monitoring, children/vulnerable persons, health, payments and public access. Record the actual instruments and powers for each supported workflow, including human-rights obligations where applicable. This register does not approve a generic nationally lawful enforcement workflow.

Choose production hosting, council/operator identities and support access from the reviewed threat/obligation model. No primary source reviewed establishes that every Australian council legally requires a separate registered platform domain or separate physical database. The owner can require stronger technical separation, and procurement may mandate it; those requirements need explicit architecture and acceptance evidence. Australian hosting alone does not decide jurisdiction, foreign access or subcontractor handling.

## Completion evidence still required

For each customer jurisdiction and workflow, the completed register must contain:

1. Governing instrument, authoritative current version, provision/control and commencement/transition date.
2. Duty holder, custodian, contracted processor/support provider and delegated decision owner.
3. Data class, purpose/collection authority, classification and applicable exceptions.
4. Approved collection/use/sharing, identity and access matrix, processing/backup/support locations and transfer grounds.
5. Record-class schedule, retention trigger/period, hold, authorised disposal and export/exit requirements.
6. Breach classification, assessment/notification rules, contractual escalation, recipients and accountable responders.
7. Required state/customer security standards and national guidance, version, assessment boundary and assurance evidence.
8. Current implemented control, evidence link, confirmed gap, remediation owner, due date and accountable residual-risk acceptance.

This initial research does not fill all those fields or constitute legal/customer acceptance. GOV-01 remains open. Functional development continues alongside this work. Production acceptance for the affected scope remains pending until the agreed jurisdiction/workflow scope is reviewed and the selected controls are implemented and verified.
